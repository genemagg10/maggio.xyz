Travel tips

eating GF on the road

Celiac and wanderlust can coexist. Here's what I've learned about finding safe, delicious food far from my own kitchen — plus notes from a few favorite stops.

My gluten-free travel kit

  • 01A translated allergy card. A clear "I have celiac disease" card in the local language does more than my accent ever could.
  • 02Find the app first. I scout GF-friendly spots before I land, not while I'm hangry at a train station.
  • 03Pack an emergency snack. Certified GF bars live in every bag. Delayed flights don't care about your diet.
  • 04Rent a kitchen sometimes. One apartment stay with a fridge resets a whole trip — and the local market is half the fun.
  • 05Learn the hidden-gluten words. Soy sauce, malt, "lightly floured," roux. The sneaky ones are worth memorizing.
  • 06Naturally-GF cuisines are your friend. Mexican corn tortillas, Thai rice noodles, Italian risotto, Japanese sashimi — lean in.
